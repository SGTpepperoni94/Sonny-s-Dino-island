# Art direction | Harbour to Skull Rock

**Target:** a bright, legible low-poly island for a three-year-old. The references get their richness from large landforms, warm and cool planes, a visible route and objects that sit convincingly on the ground. The current build has the palette and the landmarks. Its largest gap is how the land, water and landmarks fit together from child-height play.

These observations use [baseline af2a666](https://github.com/SGTpepperoni94/Sonny-s-Dino-island/tree/af2a666af4782969dfc304cb3ae5a22a0ef1ab8d) and accompany the dock change on `foundation/dock-support-and-handover`. Start with the [harbour reference](../references/pirate-harbor.png) and [skull cave reference](../references/skull-cave.png). Compare them with the corresponding [in-game presets](../../public/captures/latest/) and with ordinary play from spawn. Preset and concept lenses and positions differ. Before reshaping a whole biome, frame the concept once in the actual scene to distinguish a camera mismatch from missing geometry. Keep an ordinary play view beside it. Only the latter proves that a child gets the improvement.

## First art work order for Neon

> Work on the harbour-to-skull-cave walk at the approved revision. Start by capturing ordinary spawn, the coastal approach, the threshold and the view looking out. At spawn, compose an inviting near beach, a curving middle-distance route and a distant landmark. At the cave, shape a readable jaw opening, a visible walkable ledge and an inlet that continues inside. Block in large shapes first. Show the route in motion before spending a turn on lighting or materials. Preserve child-scale clearances, existing movement and performance budgets. Return matching before/after views and the actual changed files.

**Pass A — silhouette and playable space.** At the harbour, preserve the existing clear family pad but give its edge a shape: a low headland, a band of exposed rock under grass, and a crescent of wet sand. In the existing spawn image both branches compete against broad green ground and the volcano. Give the coastal branch a distinctive shoreline or landmark sightline. At Skull Rock, recess the passage behind two substantial cheek masses; make the warm eye a small accent above an entrance large enough to frame Sonny, the ledge and water. Check the openings with the movement and camera volumes before adding edging stones or vegetation.

**Pass B — surface, light and atmosphere.** Give the grass top, cliff face, damp sand and shallow water distinct values even when viewed in greyscale. Change large colour regions before adding fine variation. Keep sunward stone warm and deepen cooler recesses under overhangs. Let the cave threshold fall into shadow; use two warm light pools at increasing depth along the ledge. Break the current repeated white shoreline pattern into short foam runs with quiet stretches between. Tie foam to water meeting land, keep the offshore water calmer, and leave enough contrast for Sonny’s dark silhouette on the sand. Work within the existing light budget; coloured faces, vertex shading and emissive materials can do much of this work.

**Pass C — motion and reuse.** Walk the coast in both directions, enter the cave and turn to leave. Look for a cliff that becomes paper-thin sideways, a tooth that hides the player, or foam visible through stone. Test the intended touch viewport and controller camera. Compare frame-time spikes on the same machine and route before/after. A beautiful preset with a broken reverse view is still an open item.

## Direction for the other regions

| Place | Difference worth fixing | Aim for this visible change |
|---|---|---|
| Jungle | The reference makes a bend in a valley legible across several distances. In the committed view, giant foreground leaves bracket a short trail, with an animal at its crest. | Lower one bank, raise a farther shoulder, group foliage around the outside of the bend, and leave the inside open. Walking around it should reveal a new space while keeping Sonny visible. |
| Ruins | The view reads as the top of a platform with bones. The reference is a sequence of terraces a player could climb. | Stage the approach from the lower tier. Show two successive lips, weathered risers and the next turn of the ramp. Make a child able to point to the way up; inspect the ramp surface using the ground probe. |
| Volcano | The landmark silhouette works. The committed summit approach is a straight chute, with an equally straight-looking bright lava strip beside it. The reference separates a bending ridge and a recessed channel. | Open a shoulder in the ridge and reveal the lava around a turn. Let its warm light reach the nearby dark planes. From the path, the safe route and the drop should be distinguishable without moving into either. |
| Lagoon | The falling water reads, but parallel bright ribbons and repeated foam rings dominate a flat-faced basin. The reference emphasises a sheltered pool and a shelf feeding the fall. | Show the upper lip, vary the falling sheet’s width, collect the brightest foam at impact, and create a quieter shallow shelf by the dock. Check side views so the falls retain volume. |
| Title and whole island | The title is a useful harbour panorama. The high concept view communicates the enclosing island shape and the relationships between regions. | Preserve the title’s clear Play affordance. Use one separate overview to check biome silhouettes and coast spacing. Avoid distorting the world to make a title camera do the job of a map. |

## Evidence the reviewer should request

- One labelled reference, baseline and candidate triplet at the same in-game pose, FOV, aspect ratio and renderer for each changed composition. Store actual revision and camera pose. An approximate concept camera is fine if labelled.
- One continuous ordinary-camera clip with sound optional, HUD at child settings, walking in and out of the repaired region. Leave the misses and turns in. Three or four well-chosen shots plus this clip are sufficient for the slice.
- One small-screen check: without labels, can a viewer point out Sonny, the route and the cave entrance? Also inspect a greyscale thumbnail for the route becoming indistinguishable from a wall or the water. A colour comparison should be made on the same renderer.
- One explicit decision: accept the region, return a specific defect with a frame and position, or record that device evidence is missing. An overall score can accompany the evidence; it cannot resolve an obstructed path or a visible seam.

**Where to work:** `terrainMath.ts`, `terrainTrails.ts` and `IslandTerrainSystem.ts` shape the land; `terrainSplatBaker.ts` and `MaterialLibrary.ts` control surface treatment; `SkullRockCaveBuilder.ts` shapes the mouth and interior; `OceanSystem.ts` and `oceanShaders.ts` control water. Start with the smallest set implicated by the chosen shot. Keep one key light direction across the route. Tune the region locally before changing the whole island’s exposure or adding assets.
